Jira architecture & agility

Prioritization & Kanban board optimization

Separation of concerns: Class of Service on Epics (Board 2), execution by Epic and capacity bucket (Board 3), and an explicit capacity split between business and tech.

Guiding principle

No more complex double matrix. Two decisions are kept apart: how much capacity each type of work gets (a quarterly portfolio decision) and in what order items are handled inside each type (owned by one person per bucket). Strategic arbitration by cost of delay happens at Epic level; the Team board only focuses on flow.

Prioritization & capacity allocation

Comparing a tech story with a business story one by one almost always favours business, because its value is more visible. So the tech vs business trade-off is settled at allocation level, once per quarter, not at ticket level.

  • Size the incident reserve on history

    Use the average of the last 3 to 6 months, not a target. A reserve that is too small hides the real cost of run.

  • Agree the overflow rule in advance

    When incidents exceed the reserve, decide upfront which bucket absorbs it (usually BAU or tech, not committed initiatives). Without a written rule, tech always pays by default.

  • Give unused reserve back to tech

    If the incident slot is free, the team pulls a tech story. Stability is rewarded with more improvement time.

Feedback loop

Track monthly the actual consumption per bucket and the incident volume. If incidents regularly overflow the reserve, that is a data-backed case to raise the tech bucket next quarter. The tech vs business debate moves from opinion to numbers.

Key benefits

Zero JQL maintenance

Native Jira behaviour only. Container Epics make buckets visible as lanes, and nothing needs editing when a new Epic opens.

Simple pull rule

The team follows one instruction: "Respect each lane's WIP limit, then empty the top lane before moving to the next."

Tech vs business settled once

The split is decided per quarter with data, then each bucket owner ranks freely. No ticket-by-ticket negotiation.

Run cost made visible

Incident consumption is measured against a reserve, so its impact on delivery is explicit and actionable.
